The Problem

The traditional distributor model gives away too much

Working with a distributor like Cavalier is the default path — but the math shows it costs FL InStyle Thai Wine Group far more than it should, especially when we already hold exclusive US rights to the brand.

⚠️
RNDC just sold to RBG Spirit & Wine — and that's not the only problem

Cavalier is the current frontrunner because they carry Singha Beer and know Thai restaurants. But they will take 30–35% of every bottle sold forever — for work that FL InStyle Thai Wine Group can do directly, as the brand's sole US representative. We hold the exclusive rights. We bring the restaurant relationships. Yet under a traditional distributor deal, they take the margin. That's the deal we're being asked to accept.

30–35%

Margin lost to distributor

On a $300/case sale, Cavalier keeps $90–105 per case. FL InStyle gets the rest — after doing all the brand development, importing, and sales work to make GranMonte desirable in this market.

500+

SKUs competing for attention

Cavalier carries hundreds of brands. GranMonte is one line item. There is no incentive for their reps to push an unknown Thai wine when they have established brands to sell.

0

Control over restaurant relationships

Once Cavalier owns the account, the relationship belongs to them — not FL InStyle Thai Wine Group. When their rep leaves (like what already happened with the previous contact), so does the relationship. We are the exclusive US representative — those accounts should belong to us permanently.

Our Approach

Self-distribution: own the license, use a 3PL for the physical work

The US requires a licensed distributor in the chain — but that license can be ours. As the exclusive importer, we already control the supply. Adding the distributor license closes the loop — we own the entire US chain.

No other US company can import or distribute GranMonte. We are the sole gateway. The Key Insight

This is the "cloud kitchen" model applied to wine distribution

A cloud kitchen doesn't own a restaurant — it rents the kitchen infrastructure and focuses entirely on the food and the orders. We don't need to own a warehouse or delivery trucks. We rent that infrastructure from a 3PL, hold the distributor license for compliance, and focus on what we do best: representing GranMonte to Thai restaurants who want authentic Thai wine on their list.

Risks & Mitigations

Known risks — all manageable

Feasibility research surfaced six risk areas. None are blockers. All have clear mitigations.

HIGH

Temperature during Florida transit and storage

Florida heat destroys wine. Non-negotiable: refrigerated container from Bangkok, temperature-controlled bonded 3PL warehouse, refrigerated delivery trucks for last mile. Budget reefer premium into container cost from day one.

HIGH

Washington State 10% ongoing sales fee

Needs legal verification before WA market entry. If confirmed, the WA self-distribution economics narrow significantly. Do not assume WA mirrors FL — treat as a separate decision.

MED

Florida JDBW license: 90-day processing time

Apply immediately — parallel to the restaurant outreach, not after. While the license processes, LibDib (a virtual distribution platform operating in FL) can be used as a temporary bridge to any early accounts.

MED

Restaurant collections risk (net-30/60 terms)

Restaurants often pay slowly. Require 50% deposit on all first orders. Consider invoice factoring (2–3% fee) for immediate cash if needed. Build a no-fill list enforced through the distributor license reporting system.

MED

TTB Certificate of Label Approval (COLA) lead time

Each GranMonte SKU needs a COLA before it can be sold in the US. Apply to TTB now — this runs in parallel with everything else. Typical processing: 25–50 days for a standard submission.

LOW

Protecting our exclusive rights when working with sub-distributors

FL InStyle holds exclusive US representative rights from GranMonte — this must stay intact. If ever using a third-party sub-distributor, never grant them exclusive rights. Florida franchise protection laws make exclusive sub-distributors nearly impossible to terminate. All sub-distribution agreements must be non-exclusive, time-limited, with clear termination terms. Our exclusivity flows down from GranMonte; we must not pass it sideways to Cavalier or anyone else.
